# Memo: Moving the Elm Street Community Garden to drip irrigation

SYNTHETIC FIXTURE: invented people, places and figures for the usability gate.

To: Elm Street Garden steering group
From: Dana Whitfield, water coordinator
Date: 3 March
Decision needed by: 28 March

## Summary

The garden's hose-and-sprinkler watering used 412,000 litres last season, about 40% more than the two other
community gardens on the same water account. The city has raised our tier price from 1.10 to 1.65 per thousand
litres starting 1 May. This memo proposes replacing overhead watering on the 46 raised beds with a timed drip
system before the May price change, and asks the steering group to approve a budget of 3,850 and a volunteer
installation weekend on 12–13 April.

## Background

The garden has 46 raised beds (each 1.2 m by 3 m), a herb spiral, and a shared orchard strip of eight fruit trees.
Members water by hand from four standpipes, and an overhead sprinkler runs on the orchard strip three evenings a
week from June to September. Nobody tracks how long hoses run. Last July the meter showed one weekend of 19,000
litres when a hose was left on overnight at bed 31.

Two members raised complaints last season: water pooling on the gravel paths near beds 10–14, which made the
path slippery, and powdery mildew on squash and courgette leaves, which the county extension office links to
evening overhead watering.

## Options considered

1. **Keep the current system, add hose timers.** Cost about 240 for four timers. Saves perhaps 10% by stopping
   forgotten hoses, but does nothing about overhead watering, mildew or pooling.
2. **Drip lines on all beds, keep the orchard sprinkler.** Cost about 2,900. Saves an estimated 35% on beds.
   The orchard sprinkler stays the single largest user.
3. **Drip lines on all beds and micro-sprayers under the orchard trees, on two zone timers.** Cost about 3,850.
   Saves an estimated 45–55% overall, based on figures from the Hollis Road garden, which converted in 2023 and
   reported a drop from 380,000 to 190,000 litres.

## Recommendation

Option 3. At the new tier price the saving is between 280 and 340 a year, so the system pays for itself in
roughly 11 to 14 seasons on water alone. The stronger reasons are practical: no forgotten hoses, no evening
spray on leaves, dry paths, and beds that get watered during the August holiday weeks when fewer members visit.

## Risks

- **Clogged emitters.** The standpipe water carries some sediment. Filters on each zone, cleaned monthly,
  are in the budget.
- **Damage from forks and spades.** Drip lines on the surface are easy to cut. We will mark lines with green
  stakes and repair kits will hang in the shed.
- **Members who prefer hand watering.** Standpipes stay. Bed holders can close the valve on their own bed.
- **Timer batteries.** Each timer runs a season on two AA cells; Priya will add battery checks to the
  monthly shed rota.

## Decision requested

Approve option 3 with a budget of up to 3,850 from the reserve, less any neighbourhood fund grant, and confirm
the installation weekend of 12–13 April.
